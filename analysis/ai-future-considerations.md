> Inference makes sense when only one of the player mon's possible abilities could account for unexpected damage a player mon does or unexpected damage a player mon takes. In the case of such an inference, the ability can be set to the AI knowing what it is in that case. E.g. Azumarill could only do the damage it showed if it has Huge Power, or Dubwool would only take that much damage from a non-Fire contact move if it has Fluffy. Boxer's Guard will be included in this, but worth noting that I'd like the AI to factor the actual damage mitigaton Boxer's Guard would do if it either guesses or gains knowledge that Boxer's Guard is on the mon.
> Since the AI is expected to know held items, Mega Evolving inferencing is relevant. When it comes to fast kill scenarios involving a pending mega, provided the player is eligible to Mega Evolve (for now I won't take something like Tier Points into account; just whether the Mega Evolution has already been expended), outspeed fast KOs for non-commits should consider both the non-mega and the mega case, including speed changes and the influence of changing types on damage. Hidden STAB considerations for Megas have already been factored. Viability of switch-in mons will just take into account the mega for simplicity. 
> In general, baseline expansion has a plethora of score choices that lead to highly deterministic logic for certain moves when I would instead like more stochasticity in many cases, so that's something to review broadly.
